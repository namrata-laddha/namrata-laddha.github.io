I design the electronics that fly.

I am a graduate student in Astronautical Engineering at USC, working across three labs. At the Laboratory for Autonomous Systems in Exploration and Robotics (LASER) I lead the electrical and software design of two NASA CADRE-class rovers used as the experimental platform for multi-agent control research. At the Space Engineering Research Center I develop electronics and GNU Radio ground-link flowgraphs for the MAVERIC CubeSat. At the Liquid Propulsion Laboratory I build the data acquisition and instrumentation systems that capture sensor and actuator data on a liquid rocket engine test stand.

Before USC, I was an avionics engineer at Manastu Space Technologies, where the propulsion control electronics I designed flew aboard ISRO’s POEM mission on PSLV-C60 in December 2024.

My work sits where power electronics, embedded firmware, and spacecraft systems meet: schematic capture and PCB layout, EMI/EMC and environmental qualification, and the test infrastructure that proves hardware is ready to fly.

selected publications

  1. Preprint
    Design and Development of a Model Rocket with Recovery System and Thrust Vectoring for Enhanced Performance and Stability
    Shreyash Thorat, Namrata Laddha, Ninad Mehendale, and Shailesh R. Nikam
    2024
    Abs Bib HTML

    This paper presents the design, development, and testing of an advanced model rocket incorporating active thrust vectoring and an integrated recovery system. The design features a reusable combustion chamber, an interchangeable fin can, and KNSB (potassium nitrate sorbitol) as a solid propellant. Key contributions include a dual-servo thrust vectoring mechanism controlled by an MPU6050 gyroscope, a hexagonal parachute with servo-actuated deployment, and a custom Arduino-based flight computer. The rocket achieves a maximum thrust of 384 N and a total impulse of 320 N-s. Experimental results validate the effectiveness of the thrust vectoring system in maintaining flight stability and the reliability of the recovery system.

  2. Preprint
    Improving Drone Efficiency for Emergency Package Delivery through Payload Optimization and Traffic Avoidance
    Namrata Laddha, Swapnil Bhattacharjee, Om Dekate, and Ninad Mehendale
    May 2023
    Abs Bib HTML

    This study improves drone efficiency for emergency package delivery through payload optimization and traffic avoidance. By modifying the drone design to carry more payload, the platform can deliver small packages such as food, letters, and medicine over short distances. Payload optimization considers the weight of the drone and its components, reducing mass through lighter materials and a streamlined design; routing is planned to avoid congested airspace and obstacles using advanced navigation and real-time traffic monitoring. Together these changes extend flight time, reduce energy consumption, and improve the speed and safety of emergency delivery.
